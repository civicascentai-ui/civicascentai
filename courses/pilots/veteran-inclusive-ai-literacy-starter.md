# CivicAscent AI | Veteran-Inclusive AI Literacy Starter Kit (Pilot v0.1)

**Status:** Draft teaching content for review, not yet approved or delivered. No government or VA endorsement implied.

## Audience and learning outcomes
Open to veterans, military families, transitioning service members and other adult learners. No disclosure of military service or disability is required. By the end of this 45-60 minute pilot, learners can (1) describe AI's limitations, (2) draft and verify a useful response, (3) protect sensitive information, and (4) revise a job-search example into their own words.

## Module 1: What AI can and cannot do (10 minutes)
AI tools can draft, summarize, brainstorm and organize. They can also invent facts, misunderstand context or reflect bias. The learner, not the tool, is responsible for checking claims. **Practice:** Ask an AI tool to explain a familiar workplace task in five steps. Mark which steps need confirmation from a reliable source.

## Module 2: Privacy first (10 minutes)
Do not enter Social Security numbers, service records, medical information, passwords, private client data or nonpublic employer documents into a public AI tool. Use fictional examples or redact details. **Practice:** Replace a sample personal story with a fictional scenario before writing a prompt.

## Module 3: Civilian career translation (15 minutes)
Sample fictional experience: `Coordinated a 6-person team, scheduled equipment inspections and documented readiness checks.` Ask AI: `Turn this fictional experience into three clear resume bullets for an operations coordinator position. Do not invent numbers, certifications, responsibilities or results. Label anything that needs verification.` Compare every bullet to actual experience before use. Non-military alternative: `Coordinated volunteers for a community event.`

## Module 4: Verification and practical use (10 minutes)
Ask AI to propose five questions for an informational interview about a chosen field. Review for relevance, tone, accuracy and unsupported claims. Verify job qualifications using the employer's actual posting. **Practice:** Highlight one AI statement that must be independently checked.

## Module 5: Reflection and evaluation (5 minutes)
Learner answers: (1) What can AI help you draft? (2) Name two types of information not to paste into public AI tools. (3) How do you verify an AI-generated job qualification? (4) What would you change in your prompt next time?

## Instructor notes
Use a demonstration with fictional data; allow typed, spoken or written participation. Do not ask participants to reveal service history or diagnoses. Offer text-only materials, adjustable time and accessible digital documents. Avoid promises about hiring, benefits, eligibility or certifications. For benefits, medical, mental health or legal issues, direct learners to qualified professionals and official resources.

## Learner worksheet
1. My goal (non-sensitive): ____________________
2. My draft prompt: ____________________________
3. Facts I will independently verify: ___________
4. Private details I will omit: ________________
5. My revised answer in my own words: ___________

## Facilitator QC rubric
Each learner can name a limitation, recognize sensitive data, produce a non-fabricated draft and explain verification. Score each as Demonstrated / Needs practice / Not assessed. Participation is optional. Collect only minimal anonymous feedback with consent.
