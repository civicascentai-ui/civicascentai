## Starter: Practical AI Skills

**Audience:** First-time AI learners, including older adults and veterans. **Format:** Four short sessions, with print-friendly alternatives.

### Session 1: Meet AI safely
- Goal: Explain in plain language what a generative AI assistant can and cannot do.
- Practice: Ask an assistant to summarize a fictional community announcement. Compare its answer against the original.
- Check: Name one reason an AI answer might be incorrect.

### Session 2: Write and revise
- Goal: Create and edit a clear email or short document without sharing private details.
- Practice: Draft a volunteer-event invitation using made-up names and dates. Ask for a simpler version.
- Check: Identify two details a human should verify before sending.

### Session 3: Research and verification
- Goal: Compare an AI answer with a trusted primary source.
- Practice: Ask for a list of questions to research about a local public service. Confirm answers on an official website or with the service provider.
- Check: Explain why a citation alone does not guarantee accuracy.

### Session 4: Everyday work and accessibility
- Goal: Use AI for a low-stakes planning task and request accessible output.
- Practice: Make a mock appointment checklist; request plain-language, large-print-friendly text and an alternate language version.
- Check: Identify what information should never be entered into an unapproved AI tool.

### Learner safeguards
- Never enter passwords, bank numbers, private medical records, or another person's sensitive information into practice prompts.
- Do not treat AI responses as professional legal, medical, or financial advice.
- Check dates, names, facts, and links with reliable sources.
- Human instructors should offer non-digital alternatives and allow participants to opt out of sharing personal experiences.

## Facilitator companion

### Before each session
- Confirm accessible venue, seating, captions when available, and printed handouts.
- Offer large-print and plain-language copies; ask participants privately about accommodations without assuming needs.
- Use fictional examples and a demonstration account with no personal records.
- Confirm Wi-Fi and an offline backup exercise.

### Delivery structure (suggested 45-60 minutes)
1. Explain the goal and safety note (10 minutes).
2. Instructor demonstration (10 minutes).
3. Guided learner exercise (15-25 minutes).
4. Human verification and questions (10 minutes).

### Evaluation rubric
- **Independent:** Learner completes a low-risk task and verifies key claims.
- **Supported:** Learner completes the task with prompts or accessibility support.
- **Not yet:** Learner needs another demonstration or alternative format.

Record only consented, minimal, de-identified outcomes. Do not label learners by disability or veteran status in shared reports.
