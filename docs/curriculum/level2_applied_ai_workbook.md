# CivicAscent AI — Level 2 Applied AI Skills: Facilitator and Learner Workbook
Version: 2026-10-14 · Status: REVIEW DRAFT, NOT AN OPEN COURSE
Audience: adults, older adults, veterans, community participants; no prior coding required.
Delivery: four 60-minute guided sessions OR self-study with a qualified facilitator. Participants need only paper or a device; digital AI access is optional. Use fictional examples and no confidential information.
Educational guidance, not legal, medical, veteran benefits, or employment advice.

## Outcomes
By the end, a learner should be able to: (1) write a clear request using Goal/Context/Constraints/Output; (2) make a deliberate follow-up; (3) verify an important claim independently; (4) document an AI-assisted project with human judgment and a privacy check.

## Session 1: Reliable prompts (60 minutes)
**Explain (10 min).** A prompt is an instruction. A useful structure is: Goal (what), Context (who/why), Constraints (limits), Output (form). The assistant can guess incorrectly, so useful structure reduces ambiguity but never guarantees accuracy.
**Demonstrate (10 min).** Weak: “Write a message.” Strong: “Draft a three-sentence reminder for a fictional free community workshop. Audience: first-time adult learners. Use plain language, include a placeholder date, and finish with one question. Do not invent a real place.”
**Practice (25 min).** Prompt A: write a fictional reminder. Prompt B: ask for a simple comparison of two fictional workshop schedules, and ask the assistant to list assumptions.
**Debrief (10 min).** Identify a success criterion for each output; make the output shorter or simpler if necessary.
**Exit ticket (5 min).** Write one Goal, one Context, one Constraint, one Output instruction.
**Facilitator check.** Learner identifies all four fields; use a paper template if no device.

## Session 2: Guided multi-step workflows (60 minutes)
**Explain (10 min).** Break a complex task into Ask → Inspect → Improve → Verify → Save.
**Demonstrate (10 min).** Plan a *fictional* volunteer event in five steps: initial checklist → identify missing details → improve schedule → verify assumptions with organizer → present a one-page plan.
**Practice (25 min).** Have the learner make a fictional job-search preparation checklist; then request a clear table with Step / Why / Verification / Owner. Never include a real resume or service record.
**Debrief (10 min).** A good workflow has an input, output, human decision, and explicit next action.
**Exit ticket (5 min).** State two points where a human must review before use.
**Facilitator check.** The draft has a clear sequence and at least one verification checkpoint.

## Session 3: Research, sources and privacy (60 minutes)
**Explain (10 min).** AI may cite nonexistent pages, use stale information or sound more confident than warranted. Distinguish an AI claim from a source you actually opened.
**Demonstrate (10 min).** Ask about a *fictional* library's hours. The safe result is "unverified; ask the library," not invented opening hours.
**Practice (25 min).** Give three practice claims: “The class starts at 9 AM,” “All jobs require the same resume,” “Every AI answer is accurate.” For each, write a verification action and identify whether the statement can be accepted without evidence.
**Debrief (10 min).** Privacy: exclude passwords, government IDs, health records, financial accounts, DD214s and benefits information from public AI chats.
**Exit ticket (5 min).** Produce a claim/evidence/verification table using fictional facts.
**Facilitator check.** Learner labels at least one claim “unverified” and chooses an appropriate trusted source.

## Session 4: Guided project and demonstration (60 minutes)
**Project choice (10 min).** Choose one accessible fictional task: a volunteer-event plan, a simple workshop flyer, an accessible checklist, or a sample job-interview practice guide.
**Build (25 min).** Draft a prompt; save the initial result; make at least one improvement request; record one factual verification and a privacy check.
**Review (15 min).** Partner/facilitator uses rubric below. Learner explains what was accepted, changed or discarded.
**Reflect (10 min).** Finish: “AI was helpful for ___; I checked ___; a person still decides ___.”

## Level 2 assessment (learner answers)
1. Name all four pieces of a well-scoped prompt.
2. Why is a follow-up request useful?
3. The assistant invents a community center address. What should you do?
4. Should you paste a real DD214 or medical document into a public AI chat for practice? Why?
5. What two artifacts show you improved an AI result?
6. In the fictional workshop schedule, who ultimately confirms the date and venue?

### Facilitator answer key
1. Goal, Context, Constraints, Output.
2. It lets you refine/correct responses without assuming the first draft is final.
3. Mark unverified and check the center's official information with a human or official source.
4. No; use placeholders and fictional records to reduce exposure of sensitive information.
5. The original draft and an improved version (ideally with a change note).
6. The real organizer or authorized human, not the model.

## Scoring rubric (0–2 per criterion; 10 points total)
| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prompt clarity | Unclear | Some elements | Goal/context/constraints/output clear |
| Workflow | No sequence | Partial | Ordered with handoffs/checkpoints |
| Fact check | None | Mentioned | Source/method documented |
| Privacy | Real sensitive data used | Risk noted | Fictional/safe substitutions used |
| Human judgment | No decision | Vague | Clear accept/edit/reject reasoning |
Completion guide: 8/10 with no privacy breach and a learner project suitable for independent facilitator review. This threshold is a proposed internal standard, not a promised credential.

## Accessibility accommodations
Offer 18–20 pt print, optional dictation, plain text, large targets, quiet reading time, captions/transcripts for recordings, reduced-motion visuals, flexible pace and an oral response option. Allow paper completion without AI account creation. Solicit feedback without asking for sensitive medical or military records.
