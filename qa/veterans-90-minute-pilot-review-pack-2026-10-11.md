# CivicAscent AI | Veteran and Military Family AI Skills Pilot
**90-minute instructor and learner practice pack | INTERNAL REVIEW DRAFT | 2026-10-11**

## Learner outcomes
By the end, participants can (1) write a useful AI prompt, (2) check an AI answer against a reliable source, (3) remove personal details before using a tool, and (4) prepare a practical, fictional work or everyday-life draft.

## Instructor run of show (90 minutes)
| Time | Activity | Learner evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Welcome, accessibility preferences, no-account option, explain that AI can be wrong | Learner chooses paper or device |
| 10–25 | AI basics: what it can/cannot verify; demonstrate fictional example | Name one limitation |
| 25–45 | Prompt practice: role, task, context, format, constraint | One revised prompt |
| 45–60 | Verification: compare output with a primary source; distinguish fact from suggestion | Two claims checked |
| 60–75 | Privacy and safe use: remove names, contact details, service records, health data | One sanitized prompt |
| 75–85 | Practical draft: fictional appointment email or skills summary; check for inaccuracies | One corrected draft |
| 85–90 | Exit quiz, support and feedback options | Four-question exit check |

## Learner workbook: fictional exercises
**Exercise A, ask clearly:** 'Help me write a polite email asking a community center about a free digital-skills workshop. Keep it under 100 words. Do not invent dates or availability.' Revise to request a subject line and two questions.

**Exercise B, verify:** Ask an AI to explain how to find local workforce training. Identify which parts require checking on an official organization website. Do not treat AI-generated program eligibility or benefits information as authoritative.

**Exercise C, protect:** Replace this *fictional* unsafe prompt: 'My name is Taylor Sample, my phone is 555-0100, and my service record number is 123456. Write a support request.' Remove all identifiers and retain only the general purpose.

**Exercise D, practice:** Write a skills-summary paragraph for a fictional person who coordinated schedules and taught colleagues to use spreadsheets. Mark any claim the AI invented and remove it.

## Four-question exit check
1. What two details make an AI request clearer? (e.g., task and desired format)
2. Where should you verify a claim about a government benefit? (official agency guidance or qualified advisor)
3. Name one detail to leave out of an AI prompt. (personal or confidential information)
4. What should you do if AI invents a credential or accomplishment? (remove/correct it and verify)

## Instructor QC and accessibility
- Provide large-print or accessible electronic copy; semantic headings and real table headers are required in exported Word/PDF versions. This Markdown table alone is not evidence of screen-reader compatibility.
- Describe diagrams verbally, provide text alternatives, avoid color-only instructions, use captions/transcripts for media.
- Offer paper/no-account practice, quiet breaks, adjustable pacing, and a private accommodation-request contact once staffed; do not promise an interpreter without a confirmed arrangement.
- Use fictional examples only. Do not collect discharge documents, benefit records, health history, SSNs, or payment details.
- Verify any real service/benefit referrals directly against official sources before sharing.
